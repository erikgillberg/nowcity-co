Now City & Denmark

What if Salem became
the most liveable city
in America?

It is a large question for a modest city, and we ask it carefully. This is a working plan for asking it together: designed with Denmark, built for America.

Now City & Denmark · A Working Plan, Offered for Correction · August 2026
The Big Idea

A neighborhood that is a product of Denmark

Now City is building a North American platform for neighborhood-scale regenerative development, and West Salem is its demonstration district: Danish architecture, engineering, energy, water, mobility, construction, technology, and urban design working together inside one commercially replicable American neighborhood. The framing is literal: a Now City Neighborhood as a product of Denmark, the way a great piece of Danish design carries its origin as a mark of quality.

Denmark has spent two decades building institutions whose purpose is connecting Danish urban expertise to international projects: the Trade Council and its Sustainable Urban Development advisory, State of Green, the Danish Architecture Center, the Danish Design Center, DenmarkBridge. A consortium of Danish firms, universities, investors, and public institutions assembled around one American district is precisely the shape these platforms exist to support, and the concept aligns with the public priorities of His Majesty King Frederik X, whose patronages span these exact institutions. That alignment is held with care: recognition is earned through the institutions, never sought as endorsement.

The Question

A large ambition, held lightly

The question sounds immodest, so let us handle it modestly. Salem is Oregon's capital, a city of nearly two hundred thousand people on the Willamette River, an hour south of Portland. It has the raw material liveability is made from: a walkable core, a riverfront, universities, state government, and land that can still be shaped. What it has never had is a method.

Liveability is measured in ordinary days: a home you can afford near the things you love, trips that do not require a car, streets that hold public life, buildings that keep you healthy, nature that works as infrastructure, culture and play within walking distance. The cities that top the global liveability indexes score on exactly these things, and Copenhagen sits at or near the top year after year. That is not chance. It is a method, refined over fifty years, carried by a national ecosystem of designers, engineers, builders, investors, and public institutions.

We do not claim that method. We hope to borrow it honestly, adapt it openly, and give back what we learn. What Denmark's ecosystem has never had in America is the unit of proof that changes minds at scale: a whole district, designed as one system, on American soil, under American rules, at American costs. What Salem has never had is a reason for the world's best urbanists to care about it. Perhaps each is the other's missing piece. That is the hypothesis this plan exists to test.

The Model

Three what-ifs that might make one model

If the partnership works, we believe it will be because of one clean separation. We could be wrong about many details below; we do not think we are wrong about this.

1

What if Denmark backed the product?

The Danish District as a product: an integrated, exportable package of architecture, energy, water, envelopes, daylight, mobility, public realm, standards, and supply chain, engineered to American codes and costs. The coalition develops it. Danish support flows to product development, where Danish upside lives: industry product budgets, foundation missions, export-finance instruments, and the national platform institutions doing what they were built to do.

2

What if America funded the project?

Edgewater capitalizes as an American real estate project, on its own track, from American and international sources: platform capital, the district fund, and phase capitalizations. The district proceeds on its own strength. Danish partners are invited into a project that is already moving, never asked to carry it.

3

What if the project bought the product?

Procurement is the bridge. Phase One specifies Danish systems where they win on merit and purchases them at market: the demonstration is a paid installation, not a subsidized one. Every district that follows re-buys the product, and the product improves with every purchase. A channel, not a charity.

If the model holds, it can be said in one breath: Denmark backs the product. America funds the project. The project buys the product.

The Engine

What if the product had a home on both shores?

Every product needs a place where it is developed, tested, and shown. We think this one needs two, one on each side of the Atlantic, and that standing them up should be the strategy's first move.

In Salem: the Edgewater innovation district. Edgewater's plan already carries an innovation district in the studio-lab lineage of Newlab: applied research and enterprise working inside a real neighborhood. In this partnership it would become the American home of the Danish District product effort: the adaptation lab where Danish systems meet US codes, costs, and construction culture; the landing pad where Danish companies and SMEs get an American address, a demonstration block, and their first reference customers; and the measurement engine that instruments the district and publishes what the systems actually do. The product developed in the same place that buys it.

In Copenhagen: BLOXHUB. The mirror room: 600+ members working on sustainable urbanization, two coalition members in its leadership, and the natural host for the product effort's Danish side: convenings, studio sessions, and member calls against district challenges.

One product lab, two rooms, one agenda. Whether that is the right shape is exactly the kind of question the first convening should argue about.

The Team

What if two people held it together?

Here is the most honest sentence in this plan: we do not yet have the team to run it, and the plan needs that team more than it needs anything else.

Plans like this are not executed by committees. On the American side, Now City holds the site, the city, the capital, and the delivery. On the Danish side, the plan calls for a leadership team of two. One who orchestrates: someone who has delivered complex civic infrastructure inside Danish institutions, knows from the inside how public innovation happens, and can hold a room that spans ministries, federations, studios, and companies. One who leads urban strategy: someone fluent in the discipline that binds transit, land value, and daily life into one plan, because that discipline is the spine of the product itself.

Together with Now City, that team would be the partnership's executive core: the ones who take this document, sharpen it, and own it. If you are reading this and recognize the work, the document is already half yours.

The Stack

What Now City honestly holds

A fair question from any Danish partner: what does Now City actually hold, and what is it offering to do? Here is the honest inventory.

The position. Now City is master developer of Edgewater: twenty-two riverfront acres under agreement, a committed first-phase partnership, an entitlement path, and a four-phase plan across roughly a decade. That position is what makes the district a credible first customer, and it does not depend on this partnership. What follows does not scale without Danish partners; that dependence is mutual and deliberate.

Underwriting engineThe Upside Explorer: a live, driveable financial model of the district. Danish systems get priced in it, not argued about.
Product programNow City Homes and the district systems specification: the documents that decide what gets built and what gets bought.
Capital architecturePlatform capital, the district fund, and phase capitalizations, raised from American and international sources on their own track.
Coalition craftThe working method for assembling cities, institutions, and companies around a district, practiced here and reusable everywhere.
ReplicationDistricts, plural. The playbook that carries the product to the next city, and the channel that makes it compound.

The levers

Orchestration is only real when the levers are real. Now City holds six: land (what the district stands on), entitlement (what is allowed to be built), standards (what performance is required), procurement (what gets bought: the lever that purchases the product), capital design (how each phase is funded and what it must prove), and story (what the district means, told well enough that cities and capital want the next one). The Danish coalition plugs into all six without having to own any of them.

How it might scale. Salem proves the product at full district scale. The playbook, the supplier consortium, the measured results, and the coalition itself then travel to the next Now City district, where the product is bought again at version two. Denmark's export channel compounds with each district; Now City's platform compounds with each product improvement. Neither side scales without the other, which is what would make the partnership durable.

The Arc

Four acts, offered as a guess

A possible arc, in four acts. The dates are guesses; the sequence is the commitment.

Act I · Formation (now through spring 2027). The leadership team forms and takes ownership of this plan. The product lab is chartered in its two rooms: the Edgewater innovation district and BLOXHUB. The Trade Council's Sustainable Urban Development program is engaged as institutional anchor. A first Danish working visit walks the Salem site; a first Copenhagen convening assembles the coalition. The goal of Act I is not size; it is the right dozen commitments and a credible anchor engaged.

Act II · The Design Year (2027). The product takes shape against a real site: masterplan review, the city-nature and public-realm strategy, the whole-life carbon standard, system and product selection, and the mobility and urban strategy for the district's center. A Danish delegation visits Salem; Salem's leadership visits Copenhagen. The year closes with the plan exhibited publicly in both cities, and with the Phase One specification carrying the product's first order.

Act III · The Demonstration (2028 through 2031). Phase One is built and the product is delivered: Danish systems installed together, measured together, published together. Study tours run while the cranes are still up. The district becomes the American reference case Danish industry has never had.

Act IV · Replication (2031 onward). The playbook, the supplier consortium, and the standards travel to the next Now City district, and the partnership matures into something Denmark's official trade-promotion platform could recognize as its own. This is the stage at which a royal business delegation or project visit becomes appropriate to explore, through the patron institutions, at their initiative.
