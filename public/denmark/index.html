The Most Liveable City in America

A working vision for the Salem and Denmark partnership · August 2026 · Draft for coalition discussion

The ambition is simple to say and generational to do: make Salem, Oregon the most liveable city in Oregon, and in time a serious contender for the most liveable city in the United States.

Salem is Oregon's capital, a city of nearly two hundred thousand people on the Willamette River, an hour south of Portland. It has the raw material liveability is made from: a walkable historic core, a riverfront, universities, state government, and land that can still be shaped. What it has lacked is a method.

Liveability is not a slogan. It is measured in ordinary days: a home you can afford near the things you love, trips that do not require a car, streets that hold public life, buildings that keep you healthy, nature that works as infrastructure, culture and play within walking distance, and the quiet trust that a well-run place produces. The cities that top the global liveability indexes score on exactly these things, and Copenhagen sits at or near the top of those indexes year after year. That is not chance. It is method, refined over fifty years, carried by a national ecosystem of designers, engineers, builders, investors, and public institutions.

That method is exportable. Denmark exports it deliberately, through its Foreign Ministry, its national platforms, and its industry. What Denmark's ecosystem has not yet had in the United States is the unit of proof that changes minds at scale: not a product, not a building, but a whole district, designed as one system, on American soil, under American rules, at American costs.

That is what we are building, and why this partnership exists.

Why Denmark Wins Too

This plan only works if it is worth Denmark's while. We believe it is, five ways.

The showroom problem, solved. The United States is the world's largest market for urban and green-building solutions, and it buys what it can see and visit. Danish companies sell into the US piecemeal: a heat pump here, an insulation package there, a facade somewhere else. A demonstration district assembles the whole catalog into one place where an American mayor, developer, or pension fund can walk the streets and feel the result. One built district outsells a thousand brochures.

A beachhead that already exists. Danish industry is already manufacturing in America: Vestas, Danfoss, Grundfos, and ROCKWOOL all operate US plants. The district gives that industrial presence a flagship: a place where the products are installed together, measured together, and published together.

Replication is the product. Now City's model is districts, plural. Salem is the first. Every district that follows carries the same Danish systems, partners, and standards forward. For Danish firms, the prize is not one project; it is a repeatable American channel.

Alignment with Denmark's national platform. The Trade Council's Sustainable Urban Development program exists precisely to connect Danish urban expertise with American developers and municipalities. State of Green exists to tell these stories. DenmarkBridge, Innovation Centre Denmark, the Danish Architecture Center, and the Danish Design Center all carry mandates this project serves. The long-term ambition is for the partnership to earn a place on Denmark's official trade-promotion platform. His Majesty King Frederik X holds patronages across these exact institutions; the alignment with his public priorities is real, and we treat it with care: this is patronage alignment to be earned through the institutions, never a commercial endorsement to be sought.

Learning flows both ways. American economics, codes, and construction culture will stress-test Danish methods and make them stronger. What the coalition learns adapting the method to Salem becomes intellectual property for every member's next American project.

Where

In Salem: the Edgewater district in West Salem, twenty-two acres of riverfront across the Willamette from downtown and the Capitol, master-developed by Now City as a four-phase district over roughly a decade. The program already on the table reads like a Danish district brief written in Oregon: attainable Passive House-grade homes, an innovation district, a wellness institute concept centered on women's and family health, a hotel, activated public realm, and a mobility story that reconnects West Salem to the city's core. The district is real: land assembly is under agreement, the first phase partnership is committed, and the plans are public at nowcity.co/west-salem.

In Denmark: the coalition's natural rooms already exist. BLOXHUB as the convening home; the Danish Architecture Center as the exhibition venue; Living Places Copenhagen and GreenLab Skive as working reference sites; the Trade Council and Innovation Centre Denmark Silicon Valley as the state's own bridges to the American West Coast.

On the map: Salem sits on the I-5 corridor, an hour from Portland's airport, in a state whose land-use and climate policy is among America's most ambitious. It is the capital: what is proven in front of a legislature travels.

When: The Four Acts

The arc runs in four acts. Dates are targets, not promises; the sequence is the commitment.

Act I · Formation (now through spring 2027). A small working group of first movers forms the coalition: a charter, a shared map (the coalition board is already live), a formal conversation with the Trade Council's Sustainable Urban Development program, a first Danish working visit to Salem, and the first convening in Copenhagen. The goal of Act I is not size; it is the right dozen people committed and a credible institutional anchor engaged.

Act II · The Design Year (2027). A joint Danish-American design studio shapes the district's Danish layer: masterplan review, the city-nature and public-realm strategy, the whole-life carbon standard, system and product selection, and the mobility and urban strategy for the district's center. A Danish delegation visits Salem; Salem's leadership visits Copenhagen. The year closes with the plan exhibited publicly, in Copenhagen and in Salem.

Act III · The Demonstration (2028 through 2031). Phase One is built as the demonstration: Danish systems installed together, measured together, and published together. Study tours run while cranes are still up. The district becomes the American reference case Danish industry has not had.

Act IV · Replication (2031 onward). The playbook, the supplier consortium, and the standards travel to the next Now City district, and the partnership matures into something Denmark's official trade-promotion platform can recognize as its own. This is the stage at which a royal business delegation or project visit becomes appropriate to explore, through the patron institutions, at their initiative.

What formation needs first

Act I stands on five functions. Three belong to institutions: a convening room (BLOXHUB), an institutional anchor (the Trade Council), and a story platform (State of Green). Two belong to people. The coalition needs an orchestrator on the Danish side: someone who has delivered complex civic infrastructure inside Danish institutions, knows how public innovation actually happens, and can run a room that spans federations, ministries, studios, and companies. And it needs an urban strategy lead: someone fluent in the discipline that binds transit, land value, and daily life into one plan, because that discipline is the spine of the district's design year. Where those two functions are held well, coalitions of this kind succeed.

How: The Workstreams

Nine workstreams, matching the nine groups on the coalition roster. For each organization: why it wins, what its role is, how it starts, when it enters, and where it plays. For each person: the seat the plan holds open. Every role here is an invitation, written to be corrected by the person it describes.

Capital & Institutional · Capital and Standards

The scenario. Danish capital does not need to write the first check; the district's American capitalization is underway on its own track. What Danish capital brings first is standards and pattern recognition: what a people-and-planet-positive district must measure, how urban platforms are governed, and what European institutional investors would need to see before a future phase or a future district becomes investable for them. By Act IV, that dialogue has had years to mature on a real asset.

The National Horizon

Everything above is designed to add up to something Denmark itself can be proud of: a nationally significant Danish-American partnership, carried by the institutions built for it, worthy in time of a place on the country's official trade-promotion platform.

  • HM King Frederik X · King of Denmark
    Role: the horizon, held with care. His patronages span the exact institutions this plan works through, and his public priorities align with everything the district stands for. The coalition's discipline is to earn recognition through those institutions and let any royal engagement arrive at their initiative, at the right milestone, as alignment and never as endorsement.

The Invitation

Salem will not become the most liveable city in America by accident, and Denmark's method will not cross the Atlantic at district scale on its own. Both need the same thing: a coalition that treats a mid-sized American capital as the proving ground for the best urbanism in the world.
