Working Concept · Recruiting Brief

The Ten Seats

Seat specifications for the founding ten. Archetypes until real women fill them. Companion to Women & Family Health Institute.

How to Read This

Ten seats, one institution

Each seat below carries a mandate no one else at the table holds, a bar for who can hold it, and a first-year assignment that starts the day she says yes. The two keystone seats are recruited first: the insurance founder makes the model payable, and the data-and-AI founder makes its evidence valuable. Their networks help fill the remaining eight.

The Healers · Five Practices, One Lifetime
Healer · Seat 1

The Midwife

Owns the beginning: pregnancy, birth, and recovery, and the standard that the mother-child pair is one patient.

The bar
A career across settings, from home to birth center to hospital, and the standing to be believed by both mothers and medical boards.
Year one
Leads the world-model study of Finland's neuvola and Dutch home care; designs the clinic's continuous-care spine; carries the first cohort of families.
Healer · Seat 2

The Pediatrician

Owns the child's first decade, and the two-generation lens: the child's chart and the mother's are read together.

The bar
Clinical practice plus population-health fluency; someone who has seen what fragmented systems do to families and can measure it.
Year one
Co-designs the paired mother-child care record and the outcome set the whole institute will be judged by.
Healer · Seat 3

The Early-Childhood Educator

Owns the learning that happens before school ever starts, inside the clinic, the home, and the neighborhood.

The bar
Has built or led early-learning programs with measured results, and can say which cherished practices don't work.
Year one
Weaves learning into every clinic touchpoint and drafts the early-learning brief that the education venture will later build from.
Healer · Seat 4

The Family Therapist

Owns the relationships every other outcome runs through, and the institute's listening discipline.

The bar
Deep clinical practice plus systems thinking; the person in the room who notices what the family didn't say.
Year one
Designs how the clinic hears families, the listening architecture that converts exam-room insight into the research agenda.
Healer · Seat 5

The Physician for the Second Half of Life

Owns fifty, seventy, and ninety: menopause, aging, and the institute's promise that thriving has no expiration date.

The bar
Menopause and aging practice plus policy fluency; comfortable in an exam room and a legislative hearing in the same week.
Year one
Brings the UK's menopause-policy lessons home and designs the whole-life arm of the care model.
The Builders · Five Ventures Waiting to Exist
Builder · Seat 6

The Health-Insurance Founder

Owns the hardest translation: converting "thriving" into products a payer can pay for. Without this seat, the model stays a pilot forever.

The bar
Has founded and scaled a payer or payer-adjacent company; knows the actuarial and regulatory terrain by scar tissue, not theory.
Year one
Maps reimbursement pathways for continuous two-generation care and brings the first payer to the table as a design partner.
Builder · Seat 7

The Data-and-AI Founder

Owns the evidence base as an asset: community-governed, openly published, and valuable enough that the whole field wants access.

The bar
Has built data platforms or AI products at scale, with governance sophistication, and can explain her architecture to a grandmother on the family council.
Year one
Designs the community-governed data trust and the open-publication infrastructure every venture will be bound to.
Builder · Seat 8

The Fintech Builder

Owns the financial rails of family stability: saving, owning, and weathering shocks, because money stress is a health exposure.

The bar
Has shipped consumer financial products at scale and watched how real households actually use them.
Year one
Prototypes family-stability instruments with clinic families, starting from what the listening architecture surfaces.
Builder · Seat 9

The Education Innovator

Owns the path from early-learning evidence to models other communities adopt.

The bar
Has founded education ventures or networks with measured outcomes, and has scaled at least one thing that worked.
Year one
Partners with the educator seat to turn clinic practice into a replicable model with an adoption plan.
Builder · Seat 10

The Community-Development Entrepreneur

Owns the translation of research into neighborhood standards and real places, beginning with Edgewater itself.

The bar
Has delivered community development projects and speaks both zoning and equity as first languages.
Year one
Drafts the first Edgewater neighborhood standards from clinic evidence: the district as the model's first full-scale demonstration.
What All Ten Share

Proven in her field, with the standing to open doors the institute cannot open alone. Choosing mission over her next raise or her next title. Willing to publish what fails as openly as what works. Prepared to be present in Salem: this is a founding, and foundings happen in person. And holding the same conviction from ten directions: that whether women and children thrive is the most consequential design problem a place can take on.

A working concept shared for discussion within the district planning effort. The seats are archetypes; no institute has been formed and no appointments have been made. Conversations: erik@nowcitylabs.com
