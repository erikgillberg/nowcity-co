Strategy

The ecosystem of teammates and partners that realizes the Edgewater vision: who we have, how they fit, who we still need, and how we bring them in.

The Model

A master developer is only as strong as its ecosystem

Now City holds the vision, the land strategy, the master plan, and the performance standards. Everything else is delivered through partners. That makes partner selection the core competency of the platform: each phase, activation layer, and district system needs the right firm, engaged at the right moment, under a structure that keeps incentives aligned with district-level outcomes. This page is the working map of that ecosystem. We hold existing and emerging relationships with the firms profiled here; together they are representative of the caliber of partner we pursue in service of Salem and Oregon. Capital should read this page as evidence of commitment: the team and coalition are being assembled deliberately and early, so that when each phase needs its partner, the relationship, the trust, and the shared understanding of the vision are already in place. The map stays open by design: it defines how each partner integrates into the plan, identifies the roles still unfilled, and welcomes additional collaborators who strengthen the district.

The Business Bench

Four firms, many roles

We are building the district's team the way we underwrite it: deliberately, ahead of need, with alignment as the filter. The relationships here range from well developed to newly forming, ordered roughly by depth today, and each is being cultivated toward a defined role because the right partner in the right seat gives the district an edge, reduces execution risk, and protects the vision. Each firm below can play multiple positions across the district's life, and the integration strategies treat them as long-term ecosystem members rather than single-engagement vendors: a district with four phases, three activation layers, and eight-plus capital events over ten years rewards partners who commit early and grow with it. They are representative, not exhaustive; we remain open to additional collaborators of this caliber.

Established Relationship · Full-Stack Platform · Portland, OR

Ethos Companies

ethoscompanies.com · Development + Property/Asset Management + Brokerage & Capital · Relationship: Paul Del Vecchio

Our most developed relationship on the bench, held through Paul Del Vecchio, and the most complete platform: a full-stack real estate operation whose three firms cover development, operations, and capital markets, independently or as one. Ethos Development is a values-based multifamily developer and investor (ground-up and value-add, with affordable projects in the portfolio), a 1% for the Planet member whose language of human-centered design, affordability, energy efficiency, and density could have been written for Edgewater. Meritus Property Group has managed residential and commercial assets since 1991, from historic restorations to new lease-ups, across Portland and Seattle metros. Ethos Commercial Advisors is a brokerage and capital firm covering debt and equity placement, investment sales, tenant representation, and landlord representation, licensed in Oregon, Washington, and Texas.

Integration Strategy: One Relationship, Three Lanes
  • Ethos Development as phase co-GP. Their small-format, affordability-and-energy DNA fits the Scandinavian courtyard housing thesis better than almost any regional developer. Natural candidate for an attainable residential parcel in Phase 1 or 2, with their syndication and institutional JV experience adding capital reach.
  • Meritus across the whole arc. Start now: Meritus operates historic and commercial buildings, which is precisely the interim activation portfolio (400 Patterson bow-truss campus, 809 Edgewater, Now City Market support). Then phase lease-ups, where they have specific new-delivery expertise. Then long-term district property and asset management with the district-level reporting institutional capital requires. The OpCo/PropCo activation model needs exactly this operator.
  • ECA on retail and transactions. Merchandising strategy and leasing for the ~95k SF retail program and market hall tenant curation; tenant representation for innovation district companies relocating in; investment sales and debt/equity support at each recapitalization (Y4, Y6, Y8).
  • Engagement design. Approach the platform holistically so each arm reinforces the others, with lanes defined in writing. Entry points: Meritus proposal for the activation buildings and an ECA retail merchandising scope, while Ethos Development evaluates a parcel.
  • Timing. Meritus and ECA immediately (activation is live work); Ethos Development parcel conversation as Phase 1 capitalization firms up.
Advisory Relationship · Development Management · Lake Oswego, OR

Macree Companies

macreeco.com · Kyle Zaylor, Founder & Managing Principal · $3B+ project experience

Kyle Zaylor has been an active teammate and advisor to Now City, working alongside us as the district strategy has taken shape. His firm offers turnkey development leadership from napkin sketch to finished project: development project management, entitlement management, feasibility and financial analytics, owner's representation through construction, construction-defect resolution, and JV partnership as principal. Founder Kyle Zaylor has designed, entitled, and constructed over 5,000 multifamily units, 350k SF of retail, and 1.7M SF of office across California and the Pacific Northwest, with architecture degrees plus an MBA. Their stated thesis targets exactly our kind of site: complex parcels where context, entitlement insight, and design moves unlock value a spreadsheet alone would miss.

Integration Strategy
  • The integration layer. A lean master developer needs professional connective tissue between vision and delivery. Macree can serve as development manager and owner's representative for Phase 1: managing architects, contractors, budgets, schedules, and permits while Now City holds standards and strategy.
  • Entitlement engine. Their entitlement management practice maps directly onto the district-level approvals that Phase 0 must produce, working with the City of Salem and the community.
  • Quality assurance for industrialized construction. Macree's construction-defect expertise, pointed forward instead of backward, becomes proactive QA for modern methods of construction, exactly the discipline new delivery systems need to stay financeable and insurable.
  • Analytics partner. Their feasibility practice can pressure-test and independently validate the Upside Explorer underwriting, strengthening every capital conversation.
  • Timing. Already underway as an advisory relationship; the next step is formalizing a scoped Phase 1 entitlement and delivery engagement, letting both sides earn into the JV conversation.
Capital Advisory Relationship · National · $2B+ Closed

Ludian Advisors

ludianadvisors.com · Beth Mercante, Founder & CEO · Transactions $20M to $200M+

An award-winning capital advisory that structures rather than merely sources: senior debt, subordinate financing (preferred equity and mezzanine), JV and Co-GP equity through institutional and family office relationships, and credit enhancement advisory. Founder Beth Mercante's background spans KPMG, the Quantum Fund, and AIG Global Real Estate, with REBNY's Most Ingenious Deal among her credentials; the bench adds 25-plus-year lending veterans and institutional asset management depth (Prudential PRISA II). Their closed work includes a $47M mixed-use construction loan in Seattle with note-on-note and credit enhancement, a direct structural comp for Edgewater's phases. In select cases they convert success fees into equity participation, which signals appetite for exactly the platform-partner alignment we want.

Integration Strategy
  • Platform capital first. The nearest-term assignment is structuring the $50M platform raise (land basis plus working capital) with family office and institutional relationships suited to a district-scale, ten-year story.
  • Phase 1 capital stack architect. Construction debt at competitive leverage, preferred or mezzanine capital that preserves sponsor control, and Co-GP equity, with credit enhancement advisory strengthening a young platform's borrowing position.
  • A repeatable playbook. Four phases and rolling recapitalizations mean eight-plus capital events. Structuring the first well creates a template the district reuses, and gives Ludian a durable pipeline worth prioritizing.
  • Alignment structure. Explore their fee-to-carry conversion early: an advisor with carry underwrites the whole district, thinks like a partner, and defends the vision inside every capital conversation.
  • Lane clarity with ECA. Ludian leads structured capital (platform raise, construction debt, pref, Co-GP equity); ECA complements with Pacific Northwest lender depth and investment sales at recap. Define lanes per transaction in writing so both channels stay motivated and complementary.
  • Timing. Immediate and continuous through every phase.
Emerging Relationship · Development Partner · Portland, OR · Est. 1974

Rembold

rembold.com · Principals: Kira Cador (President) and Kali Bader · $1B+ developed

A newer connection on the bench, and one we are excited about: we have identified strong potential alignment and look forward to exploring it together. Rembold is a second-generation Oregon development company with 50 years of building history: 3,400+ houses and lots, 3,300+ apartment and condominium units, 580k+ SF of office, 1.8M SF of industrial, and 1.0M SF of retail. Two capabilities stand out beyond the headline numbers. First, Rembold builds and operates senior living as a repeat product line (The Ackerly communities at Sherwood, Timberland, and Reed's Crossing), giving them rare depth in wellness-oriented, age-inclusive housing. Second, The Ackerly at Reed's Crossing was delivered inside another sponsor's master-planned community, which means Rembold already knows how to build well within a framework like ours: a master developer holding the plan while a proven vertical developer delivers a parcel.

Integration Strategy
  • Phase co-developer or co-GP on residential parcels, starting with one parcel and one product type they know cold, structured under the master plan and district design standards.
  • Wellness and age-inclusive housing lead. The district vision includes a wellness neighborhood with family services on quiet green streets. Rembold's Ackerly platform extends that to aging in place, letting Edgewater serve a full life cycle and adding a proven, financeable product type to the phase mix.
  • Credibility bridge. Five decades of Oregon lender, subcontractor, and civic relationships de-risk the district in the eyes of local capital and local government.
  • Timing. An early-stage connection: relationship development first, parcel-level conversation as Phase 1 entitlement takes shape, and the strongest natural fit in Phases 2 and 3 where the wellness neighborhood concentrates.
The Design Bench

Two ODC teams, two scopes

Our Owner's Design Consultant RFP (January 2026) drew two strong multidisciplinary teams, both of whom invested deeply in understanding the district and both of whom we continue to work with. Rather than an either/or award, the working strategy assigns each team the scope it is built for: the Held Architecture coalition drives Phase 1, and the CannonDesign team leads the district masterplan and green infrastructure systems. Now City holds the OPR baseline and the interface: the masterplan sets the framework Phase 1 designs within, a shared BIM execution plan keeps models coordinated, and both teams sit in the quarterly Partner Council so the district always has one design conversation, not two.

ODC Proposal · Focus: Phase 1 · Prime: Held Architecture (WBE/SBE)

Held Architecture Coalition

heldarch.com · Kristina Held, AIA, LEED AP BD+C, CPHC, Principal-in-Charge · Oregon licensed

Held leads with Passive House-rooted, human-centered architecture: efficient envelopes, airtight prefabricated construction, and biophilic, place-based design. The decisive credential is The Cannery, a mixed-use riverfront redevelopment on the Willamette delivered with AKS through Salem's own zoning and entitlement process, featuring mass timber, geothermal systems, native landscapes, and a community wellness focus. It is the nearest true comp to Edgewater, and Held has reassembled the same coalition for us: AKS Engineering & Forestry (civil, surveying, urban land use, geotechnical, traffic, natural resources), IMEG (structural, MEP, acoustics, LEED/WELL/Fitwel, PHIUS consulting, smart-city integration), Lango Hansen Landscape Architects (Kurt Lango, Principal), and MRGC Group (cost estimating).

Assignment Focus: Phase 1
  • Phase 1 design lead. The 5.29-acre courtyard proof block is a Passive House and prefabrication problem as much as an architecture problem; Held's envelope and industrialized-construction fluency, with PHIUS consulting inside the team, matches the delivery thesis exactly.
  • Entitlement acceleration. AKS's proven path through Salem's zoning department on The Cannery shortens the learning curve on the district's most schedule-critical approvals.
  • OPR discipline. Their proposal centers Owner's Project Requirements with parametric criteria, design intent illustrations, and guardrails: the control system a master developer needs to protect quality and budget through hybrid delivery.
  • Right scale, senior hands. A compact WBE/SBE prime with principals on the work suits a single-phase assignment where speed and accountability matter more than bench depth.
  • Timing. Mobilize now on Phase 1 schematic design, OPRs, and entitlement support, designing within the masterplan framework as it settles.
ODC Proposal · Focus: Masterplan & Green Infrastructure · Prime: CannonDesign

CannonDesign Team

cannondesign.com · Lisa Petterson, Principal-in-Charge · Eric Ridenour, PM · Ashwin Toney, Lead Designer · Portland office

A 1,500-person global design firm with a 50-year Portland practice and a Living-Centered Design approach, backed by a consultant bench built for district systems: PLACE (landscape), Sherwood (water systems), PAE (energy and MEP), Holmes (mass timber structural), Century West and DKS (civil and transportation, both with prior work in the West Salem urban renewal area), RLB (cost), Glumac (facade), and ABD (acoustics). The team's shared track record is OSU-Cascades: Eric Ridenour led the award-winning Long Range Development Plan, PAE framed the net-zero campus energy concept, Sherwood led the net-zero water study, and CannonDesign delivered the district geo-exchange system (drawing on an aquifer 500 feet down through a Central Energy Transfer Station) plus Edward J. Ray Hall, Central Oregon's first all-mass-timber building, built at cost parity with steel and concrete.

Assignment Focus: District Masterplan & Green Infrastructure
  • Masterplan and framework standards. Campus-scale planning pedigree translates directly to a 22-acre phased district: land use framework, open space structure, phasing logic, and the design standards every phase partner builds within.
  • District systems architect. The OSU-Cascades geothermal, central energy, and net-zero water work is the literal playbook for Edgewater's green infrastructure ambitions; PAE and Sherwood are national leaders in exactly these systems.
  • URA-fluent engineering. Century West and DKS bring prior West Salem urban renewal area experience, valuable for aligning district infrastructure scope with URA-funded projects and city expectations.
  • Innovation district support. In-house smart-city, technology, and lighting practices support the innovation district's physical-digital layer and the landmark ambitions visible from I-5.
  • Timing. Masterplan and district energy/water studies start now so the framework is settled ahead of Phase 1 design development, and so system studies can chase Energy Trust of Oregon and ODOE grant funding this cycle.

Sourcing & Onboarding

How we find them and bring them in

The bench grows through warm paths, live forums, and engagement structures that let both sides earn trust before committing capital. Every early engagement is designed to be small, paid, and expandable.

Warm Paths First

Advisors are doors: Neal Payton to Torti Gallas and the CNU design world; Michael Shuman to community capital practitioners. Each bench firm is asked for two introductions per open role; Macree, Ethos, Rembold, and Ludian each carry deep regional networks, and referrals arrive pre-vetted.

Live Forums

International Mass Timber Conference (Portland, March) for delivery partners; ULI Northwest and NAIOP Oregon for developers and capital; SEDCOR and Salem Chamber for regional anchors; CNU for design; public-market gatherings for the market master search.

Engagement Mechanics

Paid discovery scopes for professional firms; activation OpCos as live auditions for operators; competitive preconstruction for builders; an RFQ for phase development partners once entitlement is visible; the five-move coalition sequence for innovation anchors; lane letters keeping overlapping partners complementary.

Partner Council

A quarterly convening of the whole bench. Partners who see the entire district sell it forward, spot gaps we miss, and behave like ecosystem members instead of vendors. The council is also where new candidates get introduced and socialized.

Sequencing: The Next Two Quarters
  • Immediate: ODC scope awards (Held coalition to Phase 1; CannonDesign team to masterplan and district systems) with interface protocols set; Ludian platform-capital engagement; Macree Phase 1 entitlement and delivery scope; Meritus activation-buildings proposal; ECA retail merchandising scope; land use counsel and civil bench retained; structured City of Salem URA engagement; market master and arts OpCo recruitment.
  • Within six months: Masterplan framework and district energy/water studies underway; Phase 1 schematics and OPRs advancing; preconstruction conversations with Timberlab/Swinerton and Andersen; community capital vehicle designed with the Shuman playbook; innovation coalition convening with Willamette, Chemeketa, SEDCOR, and Business Oregon; Rembold and Ethos Development parcel conversations opened.
  • Tracking for 2027: Phase 1 design development complete; GC selected through competitive preconstruction; LP and OZ equity outreach on the back of OZ redesignation; hotel feasibility with operator conversations; sports operator pilot live at 400 Patterson.
