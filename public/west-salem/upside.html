What this is, and how to read it click to expand

This is the companion to the base-case model. The underwriting model holds Phase One to a plain merchant pro forma and shows the honest result: a conventional developer building at honest Salem rents loses money, which is exactly why this corridor sits undeveloped. This page is the other half of the argument - the toolkit creative development teams use to close that gap, every lever separable and individually sized.

The waterfall is the whole story. It starts at the base-case floor and attributes the climb to five categories: cost & efficiency, revenue & placemaking, public subsidy, affordable / tax-credit equity, and financing structure. Because the levers interact, the decomposition is order-dependent (each category is added on top of the ones before it); the final all-in IRR is exact regardless of order.

Every lever is real and in use. LIHTC and HUD 221(d)(4) are the backbone of attainable housing finance. TIF, MUPTE / Vertical Housing abatement, SDC waivers, and capacity grants are standard public gap-funding tools. C-PACE, NMTC, Opportunity Zones, ground leases, industrialized construction, right-sized parking, on-site solar, and ancillary income are all levers experienced teams combine. None is a silver bullet; the thesis is that the combination, executed well, turns a -15% floor into an investable deal while keeping rents attainable.

For discussion purposes only. This is a planning tool, not an offer of securities, and several levers (LIHTC allocation, HUD sizing, TIF eligibility, OZ treatment) are subject to program rules, third-party validation, and public approvals.
