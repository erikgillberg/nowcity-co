01 · The Opportunity

An early-cycle entry into Oregon's capital

Salem is Oregon's capital and part of the Silicon Forest economy. Now City is the district's master developer, assembling a high-density, mixed-use innovation and entertainment district on an underused riverfront corridor the City rezoned for this purpose in 2022, an entry point at the start of the market's run rather than after it.

The strategy begins with a 5.29-acre mixed-use Phase One and scales across the ~22-acre district assemblage, aligned with Oregon's Silicon Forest economy and Oregon State University's research base in nearby Corvallis. The full vision and the case for Salem live in the Business Plan.

02 · The Offering

The entry point: staged platform capital

The Sponsors contribute the 5.29-acre catalyst parcel at 740 Bassett, and its appraised land value, as project equity, under a fully executed letter of intent, with definitive joint-venture agreements to follow. From there, platform capital is staged so the earliest dollars do the highest-leverage work: secure control, close on the district land, then entitle and master-plan it before any vertical build. Each stage de-risks the next, and the first money in captures the steepest part of the value curve.

Stage 1 · Raising now

~$3.5M

First-round GP equity. Secures control and funds the studies, structuring, and early design that make the district buildable.

Stage 2 · Assemble the land

$30M

$30M land basis: $25M closed for cash plus $5M the Sponsors contribute as Phase One equity, bringing the ~22 acres under single ownership.

Stage 3 · Design & entitlement

$10M

Master-plan and entitle the full district, the step that creates the platform value. See District Value Creation.

Stage 4 · Develop & hold

~$650M

The entitled district converts into a phased development pipeline that Now City develops and holds, recycling capital from one phase into the next.

Where the value is created

As master developer, Now City earns across four layers rather than a single building's margin:

  • Land value created by assembling and entitling the 22-acre district
  • Pad monetization as finished parcels are held, joint-ventured, or sold to best-in-class specialist builders
  • Development margin on the phases Now City builds itself
  • Long-term yield from holding the stabilized district as owner-operator

The full underwriting, including phase-level returns and the current Phase One pro forma, is demonstrated in the Now City Upside Explorer and available to qualified investors on request.

Opportunity Zone Position

The district parcels sit within the City of Salem's designated West Salem Opportunity Zone - one of four OZs in the city (City of Salem). Under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, Opportunity Zones are now a permanent program, with updated designations taking effect January 1, 2027 (IRS). The project entity structure is QOF/QOZB-compatible: OZ-eligible investors can pursue the 10-year basis step-up through Exit Window D, while the underwriting stands on its own without OZ benefits. We are in active conversations with OZ fund investors.

Multiple capital pathways - OZ-eligible equity, strategic equity, bridge + equity, HUD financing, and a hybrid stack - are detailed in the Business Plan capital formation strategy.

04 · Why Salem

Why Salem, why now

Nature, access, and undervalued quality of life. Salem sits at the heart of the Willamette River Valley, one of the most livable and productive regions in the U.S. - rare proximity to world-class nature, a growing innovation economy, and a lower-cost, under-recognized lifestyle advantage.

The Migration Tailwind

In United Van Lines' 49th annual National Movers Study (2025), Oregon ranked #1 in the nation for inbound movers, with roughly 65% of shipments coming into the state. It was the first time in nearly 50 years that Oregon topped the list. The destination was not Portland: Salem ranked #7 among all U.S. metro areas for inbound moves, while Portland ranked 30th. California supplied 22% of Oregon's inbound movers, with Washington, Colorado, Texas, and Montana also contributing, and nearly half of inbound households reported annual incomes of $150,000 or more (Oregon Capital Chronicle).

Portland State University's Population Research Center estimates net in-migration of roughly 17,000 residents to Oregon from June 2024 to June 2025. The pattern is movement away from the expensive West Coast metros toward the mid-Willamette Valley: higher-income households choosing livability, access, and cost. Salem is where they are landing, and where the housing has to be built.

The thesis in one line: as OSU graduates AI, robotics, and materials-science talent into Oregon's economy from 2026 onward, West Salem is positioned to house, employ, and entertain it - in a supply-constrained, renter-dominant market with by-right zoning already in place.

07 · Investor FAQ

An honest conversation about risk

We would rather earn trust than manage impressions. These are the questions serious investors ask us, answered directly, with the evidence behind each answer and the risk we are still working alongside it. We treat diligence as a collaboration: if you see a risk we have not named, we want to hear it.

"Can a market like Salem absorb a district this size?"
It is the first question, and the right one. The answer is in the phasing: roughly 1,000 homes are delivered over about a decade, in phases sized to absorption, not all at once. Phase 1 is 400 units, and later phases proceed only as demand proves out. The market it enters is supply-constrained (under 2% vacancy, 636 units of unmet demand in the core), renter-dominant (54% of households within 1.5 miles), and at the front of a migration wave: Oregon ranked #1 in the nation for inbound moves in 2025, with Salem the #7 U.S. metro. The full demand workup is in the Why Salem section and the Market Feasibility Study.

The risk we are working: if in-migration slows, later phases stretch. The rolling phasing and exit windows are designed precisely so the project is not betting on a single absorption year.
"Your team is building its first district. Why back you at this scale?"
District-scale execution is hard, and an emerging sponsor is a real risk to weigh. Now City is built as a district strategy and systems integrator that assembles experienced local and regional development, construction, and operating partners phase by phase, so execution capacity scales with the platform rather than resting on one firm. Phase One is deliberately a disciplined 400-unit block, not the whole district. Our senior advisors are load-bearing, not decorative: Neal Payton, FAIA, of Torti Gallas on master planning and entitlement, and economist Michael Shuman on local capital formation (see the Team).

The risk we are working: key-person and partner-selection risk. We mitigate it with phase-level partners and staged capital, so no single relationship is a single point of failure.
"Will the city actually approve this density?"
Entitlement risk can sink a pro forma, so we treat it as an early, deliberate stage rather than an afterthought. The City rezoned the corridor to high-density mixed-use (MU-III) in 2022, which makes much of the program by-right; the site sits within the Riverfront-Downtown Urban Renewal Area and a designated Opportunity Zone. A 2022 ULI Technical Assistance Panel engaged Salem stakeholders on this exact corridor (ULI Salem TAP). Entitling the full 22 acres is its own funded stage, the platform step detailed on District Value Creation, completed before vertical capital is at risk.

The risk we are working: design review, infrastructure conditions, and approval timing. We carry contingency and sequence entitlement ahead of construction commitments.
"What about construction costs and interest rates?"
The last few years have punished ground-up underwriting, and we will not pretend otherwise. We hold to a conservative base case rather than a promotional one. Rather than asking you to trust a single set of assumptions, the Upside Explorer lets you move rents, costs, cap rates, lease-up, and financing yourself and watch the outcomes respond. We pursue HUD and agency debt on residential phases for higher leverage and long-term fixed-rate cost, and use adaptive reuse and efficient building types (single-stair / point-access) to take cost and schedule out of the plan. The full sensitivity work sits in the Feasibility Review.

The risk we are working: cost and rate volatility are real and outside our control. Our defense is conservative base-casing, staged commitments, and financing optionality rather than one debt assumption.
"Phases 2 through 4 are not under your control yet."
A district thesis depends on assembling the land, so this is a fair challenge. The catalyst Phase One parcel is contributed by the Sponsors as equity under a fully executed letter of intent. The remaining roughly 17 acres (300 and 400 Patterson and 809 Edgewater) are held by a single owner and have been offered for sale at appraised pricing for several years without a transaction, which gives a motivated counterparty and several low-commitment control paths: option, lease-to-own, purchase, or seller participation. Control is sequenced as capital and entitlements mature, so we never carry land before we are ready to use it.

The risk we are working: assembly is not guaranteed. We mitigate it with low-commitment control structures and a Phase One that stands on its own economics even if assembly slows.
"This is a long hold in an illiquid market. How do we get liquidity?"
Duration and illiquidity are genuine costs, not footnotes. The plan is built around rolling exit windows rather than one terminal sale: a Phase 1 recapitalization around Year 4, a stabilized residential portfolio around Year 6, district components around Year 8, and an optional Year 10-plus Opportunity Zone hold for the basis step-up. Admission of the co-GP cash partner is itself a value-marking event. The full schedule is in Exit & Liquidity.

The risk we are working: exit pricing depends on future cap rates and buyer depth. The rolling structure spreads that exposure across several windows instead of one.
"What keeps you up at night, and how can we help?"
Honestly, it is the interaction of three variables we can influence but not fully control: absorption pace, financing cost, and assembly timing. We would rather pressure-test those with you than around you. We share the full model, the feasibility study, and the research behind every claim, and we invite co-investors and advisors to help us find risks we have not yet named and mitigations we have not yet built. For us, diligence is where the partnership starts.
