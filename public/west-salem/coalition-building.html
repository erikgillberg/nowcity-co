Coalition
Building

The stakeholder map for Edgewater: who holds what, why they care, and the sequence that turns a list of names into a coalition.

The Thesis

Districts are built by coalitions before they are built by cranes

Every great district precedent shares one structure: a coalition of capital, government, institutions, and community formed early, each member holding something the project needs and receiving something it values. This page maps Edgewater's coalition in six rings, from the relationships we already hold outward to institutional capital and public legitimacy. For each stakeholder the questions are the same: what do they hold (capital, land, approvals, legitimacy, talent, narrative), what do they win by joining, what is the right first move, and when. Built from the Oregon Investor and Capital Research (V3, March 2026) and extended into a full stakeholder mapping exercise.

What Stakeholders Hold

Capital, land, approvals, legitimacy, talent, or narrative. Most hold two or three; the map tracks which, so every ask matches what the stakeholder actually controls.

What They Win

Returns, legacy, deal flow, policy outcomes, community benefit, or a stage. Coalition offers are designed around their win, never just our need.

Roles in the Coalition

Champions carry the story, sponsors write checks, gatekeepers grant approvals, connectors open doors, beneficiaries give the project its public case.

Sequence

Activate the inner circle first, let bridges make warm paths, land one anchor per ring, then let the coalition compound. Cold outreach is the last resort, not the first.

The Investor Archetype We Are Hunting

A housing-focused platform that acts as GP or co-GP, deploys equity into JV structures, targets Class A multifamily at 100+ units, supports mixed-income outcomes without depending on LIHTC, and measures impact through durability, stability, and long-term asset performance. Every bridge in Rings 1 through 3 should carry this one-sentence spec.

Engagement Architecture

From map to coalition

A map is not a coalition. The mechanics below convert it: one voice, a small set of convenings that put stakeholders in relationship with each other, and a first-moves sequence that compounds.

The Ask Ladder

Advice before money, always: "help me think about who belongs in this circle" opens doors a pitch closes. Then a visit, then a pilot or introduction, then capital. Each rung earns the next.

Convenings

Three signature tables: a Mass Timber Roundtable (Ford Family, OSU, Timberlab, the design bench), a founders' briefing dinner (Rings 1 and 3), and an innovation coalition convening (city, universities, SEDCOR, employers). Hosts recruit better than emails.

Named Pilots

Give tech founders and institutions something with their name on it: a civic tech pilot, a venture studio, a research outpost, a resilience hub. Ownership of a piece beats sponsorship of the whole.

One Pipeline

Every connector works from the same Phase 1 snapshot, the same investor archetype spec, and a single tracked pipeline so introductions never collide and every conversation gets a next step.

First Five Moves (from the research, sequenced)
  • 1. Oregon Venture Fund: ask which LPs lean into place-based innovation and health; reference the Austin Foundation's public OVF commitment.
  • 2. OSU Foundation: request a joint meeting around Reser, arts, athletics, and community health; the Huang Innovation Complex gives the timing hook.
  • 3. Mass Timber Roundtable: convene Roseburg/Ford interests, OSU, GCs, and the design bench around supply chain jobs and embodied carbon wins.
  • 4. Schnitzer Properties: meet on cultural and activation strategies they have run across the West; peer respect first, capital later.
  • 5. Expensify: ideate food hall small-business tenancy and fintech rails for local vendors; they have done it in downtown Portland.
Cadence & Sequencing
  • Now: activate all Ring 1 connectors with the snapshot and archetype spec; City of Salem and Polk County structured engagement; OVF and OSU Foundation meetings; Grand Ronde relationship opened properly; Urban Catalyst sanity-check call.
  • Next two quarters: the three convenings held; Tokarski/Mountain West co-GP conversation advanced; two anchor-family conversations live (Ford and Reser angles first); grant calendar built and first stack (brownfield or resilience hub) filed.
  • By Phase 1 capitalization: one anchor family publicly engaged, one OZ platform in diligence, public stack committed alongside private capital, and a standing community table that makes every approval hearing easier instead of harder.
