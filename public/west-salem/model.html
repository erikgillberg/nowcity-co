What you are looking at, and the assumptions behind it click to collapse / expand

This is a live underwriting model for Phase One of the Edgewater district: the 5.29-acre first phase at 740 Bassett St NW. Every control on the left feeds a month-by-month engine running from April 2026 through exit. It mirrors our Excel pro forma: land and pre-development spend, construction draws, a construction loan with capitalized interest, a permanent refinance, lease-up, operations, and a sale roughly two years after opening. Nothing is precomputed. Each adjustment re-runs the entire model in your browser, including the sensitivity grid.

The base case is the company's June 2026 underwriting on the current 383-unit, three-block design: $315/SF residential hard cost plus 10% contingency, $10.9M land all-in, construction debt at 7% with a 65% loan-to-cost target, a 30-year permanent refinance 18 months after completion, 95% stabilized occupancy after a 12-month lease-up, and exit pricing at a 5.0% residential and 7.0% commercial cap rate on forward 12-month NOI.

Two financing cases. The Merchant case is conventional construction debt rolling to a 30-year perm with a sale about two years after opening. The HUD / LIHTC case models a single FHA 221(d)(4) construction-to-perm loan with 40-year amortization, a Davis-Bacon prevailing-wage premium on residential hard costs, a LIHTC affordable sleeve with rent discounts and credit equity in the stack, and conversion 12 months after opening. Both are indicative structures for comparison, not committed terms.

Held constant (deliberately not on sliders): the development calendar beyond construction duration, soft-cost ratios (~14.9% of hard costs), the 45,280 SF commercial program at $32/SF, 200 parking spaces, 1% bad debt, and a 3% in-kind sponsor contribution that funds the development fee.

Read with care. Base rents carry the prior unit schedule's $/SF onto larger units and have not yet been validated against Salem market comps. That is the single assumption most worth stress-testing here. The model also excludes things that sit outside Phase One underwriting: Opportunity Zone tax benefits, the interim activation businesses, and district Phases 2 through 4. Returns shown are project-level, before GP/LP waterfall splits and before taxes.

For discussion purposes only. This is a planning tool, not an offer of securities. All figures are estimates subject to change with site control, design development, and due diligence. Questions or a scenario worth keeping? Export it and send it to erik@nowcitylabs.com.
