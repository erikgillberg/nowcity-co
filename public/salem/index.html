Salem, Oregon · The Willamette

A river district
on both banks.

Salem has a river running through the middle of it and very little built to face the water. This is a proposal to change that, on both sides at once, and the work it would take to find out whether it holds up.

Now City · A working proposal · September 2026
Who We Are

A small development company working on one question

Now City is a development company based in Oregon. We work on neighborhoods where housing, work, food, care and movement are close enough together to make an ordinary day easier. We have been working in Salem since 2024, on land on both banks of the Willamette, and this site is where the thinking is kept.

What we do

We assemble sites, plan districts, and build. We are the party that carries development risk and stays for the long term, which is a different job from designing a plan and handing it over.

Where we are in it

Early. We have land positions and a plan, and no entitlements yet. Nobody named on this site has committed to anything. Most of what follows is a set of arguments looking for people willing to test them.

Why we are writing this down

A district is not something a developer decides. It needs a city, a region, employers, institutions and the people who live nearby. Publishing the thinking early is how those conversations start with something concrete.

The Vision, In Short

One district, two banks, and a reason to cross

Salem's riverfront is mostly industrial, fenced, or turned away from. The two sides of the river do not give each other much reason to visit. The proposal treats both banks and the water between them as one district, joined by a rail bridge that already carries people on foot and on bikes.

The east bank

The riverfront side of a downtown that already exists. Homes that face the water, a continuous public edge along the bank, and ground floors that keep the evening going a few minutes from the Capitol.

The west bank

The working side, kept working. Industrial buildings adapted into workshops, studios and lofts, with new homes built in beside them, so the making stays instead of being cleared out for housing.

What it is for

Homes at a range of prices near the jobs the region is already adding, a riverfront the whole city can use, and a place a young person educated here has a reason to stay in.

The longer version, with the plans and the open questions, is on the District Vision page. The technology and employment argument is on the Innovation District page. What daily life is meant to feel like is on Placemaking.
First Steps

What we are actually asking for now

None of this should be believed until it has been studied by people with no stake in the answer. The near-term work is not construction. It is three studies, a table of partners willing to sit at it, and one building brought back into use.

One
Commission the studies

Market and workforce, site and district feasibility across both banks, and whether a building-components manufacturing base holds up. Independently commissioned and published in full, whichever way they come out.

Two
Convene the table

The City, the region and State, employers, the universities and colleges, and the neighborhoods. Each holds something the others cannot supply, and most of the work here belongs to somebody other than us.

Three
Prove it with one building

Adaptive reuse first, because it is faster, cheaper to finance and lower carbon than new construction, and because an occupied building is more persuasive than a rendering.

Now City is not an eligible applicant for most of the public funding that exists for study work, and that is appropriate. Public money should not pay a private developer to study its own site. What is needed are public and institutional sponsors who can hold the work, commission it independently, and publish it.
What We Are Designing For

Designed for everyday vitality

Now City's mission is to accelerate the transition to regenerative, resilient and equitable communities and cities.

Regeneration

Restore the natural systems that sustain daily life, clean air, water, soil and food, improving personal health while rebuilding ecological function at the neighborhood and district scale.

Resilience

Homes, mobility, energy, food and social systems designed to perform under stress, climate and economic alike, strengthening household security and community-wide stability.

Equity

Designing around caregiving, childhood, aging, mobility independence and access to opportunity improves daily life for individuals while creating more stable and connected communities for everyone.
