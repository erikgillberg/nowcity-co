Leadership

Two founders, one synthesis

Now City's founding insight comes from combining the operating reality of property with the systems discipline of technology: districts treated as living systems, where capital, design, and operations are engineered together from the start.

Co-Founder & CEO

Erik Gillberg

Erik has spent 30 years inside the operating reality of property: how buildings live, age, and either serve or fail the people in them. Alongside it he built a 20-year career in technology, business development, and startup leadership, learning to design systems and align teams around outcomes rather than tasks. Now City is the synthesis of the two.

As master developer of Edgewater, Erik's role is holding the whole: a vision worth committing a decade to, capital structures that let attainable homes pencil, and partnerships aligned enough to survive four phases of delivery. His operating principle is care, for residents, for partners, for the river, and for Salem, backed by the conviction that development done this way is the strongest financial strategy available.

Co-Founder & COO

Ritchie Ju

Ritchie leads design, development, and operations. He holds a Bachelor of Architecture from Carnegie Mellon, a Master of Science in Architecture and Urban Design from Columbia, and is completing his MBA at Johns Hopkins. His career spans urban design, mobility, real estate development, and entrepreneurship, including the team that led Citi Bike's expansion at Lyft, capital raising and development with a visionary NYC mixed-use developer with $3B+ in total project capitalization, and co-founding a 7-figure ecommerce business.

Growing up between New York and Beijing showed Ritchie how vibrant, walkable districts improve quality of life. That experience became the foundation for Now City's mission: beautiful, sustainable districts where people live, work, and gather within walkable communities.

Operating Model

A small core, a scaling bench

Now City operates as a district strategy and systems integrator, supported by a growing bench of development, construction, infrastructure, and operating partners. Early phases are executed by experienced local and regional teams with direct market knowledge, while Now City maintains continuity of vision, performance standards, and long-term district control. As the platform scales, execution capacity scales with it, through joint ventures, operating partnerships, and institutional collaborators aligned with district-level outcomes.

For a platform investor, this is the point: the $3M builds a lean company whose leverage comes from systems and coalitions, and whose execution capacity is rented from proven partners until owning it is earned. Payroll stays small while the platform's reach compounds.
