01The Opening

America needs districts. Almost nobody is set up to deliver them.

The country is short roughly four million homes, and the shortage is sharpest where people actually want to live: walkable, connected neighborhoods near jobs, nature, and daily life. Mid-sized cities hold the land, the zoning momentum, and the demand. What they are missing is the actor.

1

The demand is real

Oregon was the nation's #1 inbound state and Salem the #7 inbound metro in United Van Lines' 2025 National Movers Study. Salem's typical renter pays about a third of income in rent, roughly half of renter households are cost-burdened, and vacancy near 5% is set to tighten as the construction pipeline thins sharply. The pattern repeats across the mid-sized West.

2

The policy window is open

Cities have rezoned corridors for density, tax increment tools are proven, the Opportunity Zone program is now permanent, and single-stair mid-rise reform is opening across the West Coast, jurisdiction by jurisdiction.

3

The role is vacant

Merchant builders build one building and leave. Institutions arrive after de-risking. The master developer role, the actor who assembles, entitles, and stewards the whole, is unfilled in nearly every mid-sized market.

Now City exists to fill that role. We are a vertically integrated master developer and operator: we help communities unlock the value in underused land, treat the district as one living system, and steward the result for decades. The deeper reason the model works is structural. Construction, infrastructure, capital, and operations are normally optimized separately, by different firms, on different clocks, with different exit horizons, so the gains that live between the layers go unclaimed: a high-performance envelope raises capital cost and lowers operating cost for decades, and a merchant builder cannot capture that trade because they sell before the operating years arrive, so it gets value-engineered out. Now City holds. That single fact stacks three efficiencies no single-layer actor can reach: capital efficiency from the blended public-private stack, resource efficiency engineered once into the chassis and district systems, and time efficiency from industrialized, repeatable delivery. Districts built this way outperform, and the platform that can repeat them compounds.
01bMission & Method

Aligned with what people pay for

Our mission is to accelerate the transition to regenerative, resilient, and equitable communities. Not warm fuzzies: these are the outcomes residents pay a premium for, commercial tenants seek out, and institutional capital is increasingly mandated to hold. Little of it needed inventing. The technologies exist, and most people are rightly impatient that they remain so rarely deployed. Our work is integration: pull the pieces into one product, get it financed, get it built, and stay to help it grow. Four layers, engineered together, in different stages today.

Layer 1

Regenerative placemaking

Every place is a living system: land, water, people, culture, history, already becoming something. We begin by learning that story, then design so the neighborhood adds life to it, and so the people who live there gain capacity to keep it thriving.

The measure is not less harm. It is a place, and a community, more alive every year.
Layer 2

Green infrastructure

A neighborhood has a metabolism: energy, water, nutrients, materials. We are designing that metabolism at district scale, where the loops can close: energy made and stored locally, water cycled, soil and canopy sharing the work of pipes.

Unlike pavement, living infrastructure can gain value as it matures.
Layer 3

Industrialized construction

Homes are among the last major products still made largely by hand, on site, one at a time. The last wave of factory building failed on demand, not technology. Our strategy runs demand first: standardize one design, aggregate orders across our districts and our partners', bring that volume to factories that already exist.

Where practiced at scale: months faster, meaningfully cheaper, with the precision that makes performance attainable.
Layer 4

Innovative finance

Much of what is wrong with development traces to a mismatch: neighborhoods create value over decades, while the money behind them demands its exit in years. We are building structures that blend public purpose, patient private money, and community participation.

Each paid in the currency it seeks, with value shared where a place creates it.
Why now. The built environment is being digitized, and capital has noticed: in July 2026 a physical-AI company raised $1.7B led by Andreessen Horowitz† to digitize and operate the physical world. Districts are where that arc lands: the demand, the deployment environment, and the operating data, in one place with one long-term owner. Now City is built for it, instrumented from day one, with open integration points. Physical AI makes this company more scalable and more valuable. It does not make it worth doing; the mission does that.
02The Platform

One company, built to repeat the hardest role in real estate

A master developer earns across four layers: land value created by assembly and entitlement, pad monetization to phase partners, development margin on the phases it builds, and long-term operating yield on the district it holds. The first three fund the journey; the fourth is the destination. Now City's edge is that every layer runs on systems the company owns.

Layer 1 · Land value

Assembly, entitlement, and master planning turn fragmented parcels into an investable district. The step change in value accrues to positions the platform structures.

Layer 2 · Pad monetization

Finished, infrastructure-served parcels are held, joint-ventured, or delivered with phase partners, converting entitlement work into proceeds and aligned partnerships.

Layer 3 · Development margin

On phases Now City builds: development fees during delivery plus promote at stabilization, underwritten live in the Upside Explorer.

Layer 4 · The destination

The stabilized district is held as owner-operator and recapitalized rather than sold. Recurring operating income, compounding value, and the operating data that improves every district after it: this is where the model is headed, and section 04 is how it earns.

03The Product Engine · Now City Homes

The technology to build great housing in factories already exists. What's been missing is an anchor buyer.

The U.S. has spent decades trying to fix the factory. The actual problem is demand certainty: factories cannot survive on project-by-project pipelines. Industrialized construction works when an anchor buyer provides volume certainty, and the evidence is consistent wherever it appears.

~10,000
Public Housing Sweden · Kombohus

Apartments built or in production from a standardized catalog: on average five months faster and 20-25% below comparable construction cost (Sveriges Allmännytta). The anchor-buyer model, proven at national scale.

~$20B
U.S. Department of Defense

A nearly $20B annual construction budget beginning to move toward factory-built delivery: the Defense Innovation Unit's industrialized-construction initiative targets 30% faster delivery at 20% lower cost.

<7%
The lesson of the last cycle

Offsite construction remains a single-digit sliver of U.S. starts, roughly 6-7% even after a decade of growth (Modular Building Institute), and Katerra burned through ~$2B of venture capital before its 2021 bankruptcy. Capital alone cannot force this market; demand certainty can.

One principle, two starving systems. The last wave of factory building failed for lack of committed orders; most development stalls for lack of committed residents. Those are the same problem, so we run demand first on both sides. On the supply side, the order book below. On the resident side, we are learning from the best product companies: form the community before the buildings, invite future residents into the design, and let reservations and commitments lead construction the way orders came to lead production in the car business. Demand secured early softens the factory's risk, the lender's, and the lease-up's at once.
Now City Homes is our strategy to become that anchor buyer for the private, regenerative market on the West Coast. We aggregate demand across our own districts and partner pipelines, standardize the product, and deliver it through factory partners. The pipeline creates the certainty; the certainty creates the economics.
Now City Homes
Standardized chassis. Localized soul.

One chassis, a full catalog of neighborhoods

The shell, core, structure, and factory tooling stay constant. Only the interior mix and the exterior expression change. A standardized core delivers the cost, schedule, and quality; the contextual skin means standardization never means sameness.

Typology 01 · The Hero Chassis

The Now City Block

A standardized single-stair mid-rise that flexes its interior mix, from compact homes for students and seniors to 3- and 4-bedroom family units, without changing the shell, core, or tooling. Stands alone or chains into a courtyard block: four stories under Oregon's current code, six under Seattle's, with the taller tiers following each jurisdiction's reform.

Typology 02 · Missing Middle

Now City Row Houses

A door-on-the-street typology for the peri-urban edge, with an integrated ground-floor ADU and separate entrance for multigenerational living, supplemental income, or aging in place. The same envelope and delivery system, reformatted.

Typology 03 · The Density Anchor

Contextual Urban Tower

Targeted height where a district needs it, sited to protect neighborhood light and views, never tower-next-to-tower. Shared social floors interweave the vertical stack so density scales without isolation.

The regulatory window, opening jurisdiction by jurisdiction

Single-stair reform is arriving city by city and state by state rather than all at once. The catalog is engineered to move with it: buildable under Seattle's code today, code-ready for each jurisdiction as it opens. First movers with a code-tested chassis capture each opening as it lands.

Seattle

Single-stair apartment buildings up to six stories permitted since 1977. A 2025 Pew study of every fire death in Seattle and New York, 2012-2024, found single-stair buildings matched other residential buildings, with no death attributable to a missing second stair.

Washington State

Statewide single-stair directed in 2023 (SB 5491) and adopted into the 2024 state code, effective May 2027; Vancouver, WA builds under Seattle-style provisions now. 2026 laws added smaller elevators (SB 5156) and first-in-nation scissor-stair reform (HB 2228).

Oregon · four stories, city by city

The 2025 state code's Appendix Q allows single-stair up to four stories, four units and 4,000 square feet per floor, in cities that adopt it with fire-service approval; Portland adopted in late 2025. Taller tiers await a state amendment, expected to be taken up in 2027.

California · opening

The State Fire Marshal's mandated report arrived in March 2026, recommending a four-story cap with added safeguards; enabling legislation has not yet advanced. The catalog will be ready when the market's largest state is.

03bThe Order Book

Demand certainty is manufactured. We build it four ways at once.

A factory believes an order book, not a vision. Now City builds one the way an airline fills a route: a committed base load it controls, anchor customers on framework agreements, a distribution channel that converts continuously, and chartered volume from partners who need thousands of units on committed schedules.

1 · The base load we control

Edgewater's ~1,000 homes across four phases, specified, sequenced, and scheduled by the master developer itself. Every district the platform wins adds a thousand-home block to the book. No sales cycle stands between the platform and its own demand.

2 · Anchor customers on framework terms

The Kombohus move, translated to the West Coast: cities, housing authorities, universities, and employers adopt a pre-engineered, pre-priced catalog under framework agreements, aggregating many small public buyers into one order. The coalition method is already opening these doors district by district.

3 · The underwriting channel

Every external deal team on the Upside Explorer is a qualified buyer in formation: the software proves, in their own numbers, that the chassis is the move that makes their project pencil, then routes the order to the catalog. Distribution for the software is aggregation for the factory.

4 · Chartered pipeline volume

Framework supply agreements with West Coast transit-oriented developments and large master-planned communities: buyers who need thousands of standardized, high-performance units on committed schedules, and who gain factory economics they could never aggregate alone.

Why the book converts: the catalog arrives pre-engineered (the Passive House chassis), pre-approved (code-tested jurisdiction by jurisdiction), pre-priced (framework factory terms), and pre-proven (every delivered building becomes the comp that sells the next order). Buyers commit to certainty, and certainty is the product.
04The Operating Layer

The district gets better after it is built

Merchant development ends at the certificate of occupancy. Now City's model begins compounding there: the hardware keeps performing, the software keeps optimizing, and the experience keeps earning. Holding is what makes all three collectible.

Hardware keeps performing

Passive House-level envelopes, the standardized chassis, and commissioned district systems are engineered once and then measured for decades: metered energy and water actuals against design intent, maintenance against plan.

Software keeps optimizing

Adaptive Urban Intelligence™ at nowcity.ai closes the loop: operating data feeds chassis revisions and the next district's underwriting, so the model's forecast error narrows with every project it touches.

Experience keeps earning

Programming, ground-floor vitality, and operating discipline are designed to earn a retention premium: lower turnover, less downtime, fewer concessions. External evidence supports it; Now City data does not yet exist, and we label it accordingly.

What compounds, specifically

The loop is explicit: operating data feeds chassis revision, and chassis revision shows up in the next district's cost, schedule, or net operating income. Each variable has a named mechanism and a named instrument.

05bThe Long Arc

Why this is bigger than a developer

We stress-test the plan with one question: what would it take to build and operate one hundred thousand homes this way over thirty years? That is AvalonBay scale: one of America's largest apartment REITs reached roughly 97,000 homes over three decades of building and buying, with a REIT-scale balance sheet behind it5. Averaged, the question demands more than 3,000 homes a year for thirty years, a pace few institutions have ever sustained, and the largest developer in America started 8,247 units in its best recent year6. A developer alone reaches that scale only by becoming a REIT. That finding is why the platform is built as a product and operating company rather than a development company.

Including homes Now City did not build. Operating is the engine that touches every unit, earns recurring revenue, and feeds the learning loop in section 04.

08Honest Questions

The questions we would ask before writing this check

We would rather earn trust than manage impressions. Direct answers, with the risk we are still working named alongside each.

Concentration

"Is this just a bet on one project in Salem?"

Today, Edgewater is the platform's main engine, and this raise is sized to its milestones. The position you buy is broader: Now City Homes and the software have their own commercial paths, the second district search is funded in this budget, and the playbook is the durable asset. The risk we are working: near-term value does concentrate in Edgewater's progress; the milestone ladder converts that concentration into proof quickly.

The Katerra question

"Didn't industrialized construction already fail?"

Capital-first modular failed; demand-first modular works, from Sweden's Kombohus to the DoD's current program. Now City Homes is demand-first by construction: the anchor pipeline exists before factory commitments, and delivery runs through established factory partners with zero proprietary factory CapEx. The risk we are working: partner capacity and pipeline conversion are execution work, and we treat them as such.
