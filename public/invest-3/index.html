Now City · Slide 1 · The Vision

The neighborhood is the next platform.

Computing became cheap and universal when hardware, operating systems, and applications became one product. The built world never got its platform: every project is still bespoke, built by temporary teams, and handed off the moment it starts working. We are building the platform, shipped as complete neighborhoods.

Slide 2 · The Macro Problem

Structural friction in the world's largest asset class

1
Productivity never arrived. Construction productivity has been roughly flat for decades while nearly every other industry compounded.1 Housing is still artisanal: designed once, built by hand, never versioned.
2
Demand moved; supply cannot follow. 78% of Americans say they would pay more for a walkable neighborhood,2 and the industry has no repeatable way to make one. The climate-resilient mixed-use sector is a ~$100B U.S. market with no product platform serving it.3
3
The megaproject distraction. Billions go to smart-city moonshots that fail because the city is too big a unit and the building is too small. The unit that works is the neighborhood.

"Cities are a network of neighborhoods."Kent Larson, MIT City Science

Slide 3 · The Solution

One chassis. Modular attractors. A city's own soul.

1
The standardized chassis. One housing and infrastructure platform, engineered once for health, energy, and cost, designed for factory delivery. Sweden proves the play at national scale: ~10,000 apartments, five months faster, 20-25% below comparable cost.4
2
Neighborhood OS. The intelligence layer that underwrites the district today and runs it for decades: energy, water, mobility, maintenance, community. Its forecast error feeds back into the product, so every neighborhood improves the next.
3
Modular attractors. The attachments that make each district a destination, fitted to each city: an innovation anchor, sports and wellness, food and market halls, the waterfront. Same platform, different city, different soul.

Slide 5 · The Strategy

Five years building the foundation first

1
Position, quietly. Since 2021: a rezoned riverfront corridor secured with the landowner investing $5M under a signed LOI instead of selling; verified Opportunity Zone; a civic coalition assembled before capital was at risk; a Danish design-and-delivery channel in formation.
2
Product before promotion. The housing catalog, the underwriting engine that becomes Neighborhood OS, and a documented public-private capital playbook, all built on the live district, not in a lab.
3
The acquisition engine. Operating capability gets bought, not improvised: the roadmap's first major move after proof is acquiring a regional management platform, adding capability, cash flow, and data in one transaction.
4
The shared moat. Every district feeds one set of standards, vendor relationships, capital partners, and operating data. The platform gets stronger with each deployment; a competitor starts from zero.
Slide 6 · Business Model

Scaling real estate at the speed of product

1
Develop. Master developer and development fees, promote, and land value on owned districts. Edgewater models ~$39M in platform fees over its first decade.5
2
Productize. Per-home margin and licensing as the catalog ships into other developers' projects, with demand aggregated across the network.
3
Operate. Recurring software and management revenue on everything the platform touches, compounding with units under management, the layer where real estate multiples become technology multiples.
