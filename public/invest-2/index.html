01The Category

A master developer and district operating platform for the physical AI era

The economy is entering a physical AI phase: intelligent, adaptable systems embedded in the real world, from energy and mobility to construction and logistics. Enormous capital is flowing to the machines. Far less is going to the scarce, local, difficult work of forming the districts where those systems actually get deployed, operated, and paid for. That work is Now City's.

The thesis is being validated at scale. In July 2026 a physical-AI company raised $1.7B to digitize and operate the physical world, an independent bet that land, real-estate construction, energy, and cross-stack competence are where this economy is won. We read the same signal and draw the opposite conclusion about our own posture: Now City should not retreat into a narrow complementary role. It should deepen its ownership of district formation, software, data, and physical-systems economics, and remain free to partner, build, buy, or invest as value dictates.

Develop districts

Land strategy, site control, entitlement, master planning, capital architecture, public-private alignment, phasing, and long-term stewardship. Value compounds as land, entitlement, and development margin accrue to positions the platform structures.

Deploy physical systems

Design standards, procurement, systems integration, pilots, commissioning, and data rights across construction, energy, water, mobility, food, waste, and robotics. Now City creates the demand and the real-world environment that physical systems need.

Operate the platform

Software, analytics, digital twins, asset and district workflows, resident and operator services, performance measurement, and continuous optimization. This layer produces recurring revenue and long-duration data relationships.

03The Doctrine

Own the control points. Integrate the ecosystem. Build or buy where ownership earns it.

Complementarity is a choice, not a constraint. Now City owns the scarce control points that make the district work, integrates the best of the ecosystem around them, and stays free to build, acquire, or take a stake when the economics justify it.

Control points we do not surrender

  • The master-developer and customer relationship.
  • Land strategy, site rights, entitlements, phasing, and district standards.
  • Capital architecture and the right to participate in project and operating economics.
  • The district data model, data rights, benchmarking, and source traceability.
  • The software interface used by deal teams, operators, residents, and partners.
  • Procurement standards, vendor qualification, and systems-integration decisions.
  • Civic, community, institutional, and landowner relationships.
  • The performance framework: what is measured, verified, improved, and financed.

Where we partner, build, or buy

Industrialized construction, district energy and water, mobility and shared fleets, food and resource-recovery businesses, and specialized robotics are integrated with specialist partners first. Ownership becomes attractive only when repeat volume, utility-like cash flow, or a repeated district-specific job creates durable margin and Now City holds privileged demand or data. Software and data are the exception: they are a core control point, owned from the start, because they are the source of cross-district learning, recurring revenue, and strategic leverage.

The test we apply: does it control the district customer, data, standards, or economics; will it repeat across districts; does integration risk decide whether the district works; could an outside provider commoditize us; and would ownership consume capital before demand is proven? The answers decide own versus partner, case by case.

07Direct Answers

The questions that decide this

"Are you a consultant or a developer?"

A developer. Now City is master developer of Edgewater West Salem, holding land strategy, plan, phasing, partnerships, and district standards under an executed sponsor LOI. The platform and advisory work extend that execution to other districts; they do not replace it.

"Do you build technology and own infrastructure?"

We own the software and data as a core control point, and build it from live district work. Physical infrastructure and construction we integrate with specialist partners first, and own or take a stake only where repeat volume or utility-like cash flow earns it. We do not vertically integrate ahead of demand.

"What is the status of West Salem?"

Advancing with the Phase One land sponsor under an executed LOI; due diligence, capital formation, entitlement strategy, and delivery-team formation are underway. Phase One begins on 5.29 acres within a roughly 22-acre, four-phase vision. Definitive JV, land contribution, and construction remain subject to diligence and financing, and are labeled as such.

"How do you work with other master developers?"

Through a District Partner Program that begins with one hard decision on a real project and can expand into integrated pre-development, physical-systems deployment, managed operations, or co-development. Early conversations with regional developers are exploratory and are not described as partnerships until roles and permissions are clear.

"Are you affiliated with any physical-AI company?"

No. The large physical-AI companies are independent, and their capital and thesis validate the importance of land, construction, energy, and cross-stack competence. Now City is an independent company that owns district formation, software, and operations, free to partner, build, buy, or invest where value dictates. We do not imply a relationship that does not exist.

"How do you protect resident and district data?"

Data is portable, permissioned, auditable, and usable for district-level optimization, with source lineage recorded so credibility is a product feature, not only a communications practice. Core data rights and platform IP stay in the parent company and are licensed to project vehicles and partner districts.
