The Problem

Conventional underwriting says the good project does not pencil. It is wrong, and that error leaves good projects unbuilt.

At honest, attainable rents, a conventional building on a conventional cost basis loses money. Capable teams close that gap every day, with a stack of moves that stay invisible to the investors, lenders, and partners whose yes a project needs. The Now City Upside Explorer makes that case visible, credible, and interactive, so the people who decide whether a project gets built can see exactly how it works.

How It Works

From the honest floor to the deal that gets built

Start honest

The base case earns nothing it cannot cite. Rents, unit sizes, and caps are grounded to achieved local comps. The floor is allowed to be negative.

Pull real levers

Building type, industrialized cost, placemaking, public tools, tax-credit equity, agency debt. Each is a separable, underwritten number, not a fudge.

Read the build-up

A waterfall attributes every point of the climb to a named category, so the result is legible, not asserted. Toggle eight worked scenarios.

Export & share

Send a scenario to anyone, or rebuild it as a branded, industry-standard Excel model with live formulas. One source of truth for every stakeholder.

Glossary

Plain-language definitions

Every concept and acronym the tool uses, in one place. Hover any dotted term elsewhere on the page for the same definition.

Point Access Block (PAB)
A multifamily building where apartments cluster around a single central core (one stair, one elevator) instead of lining a double-loaded corridor with two exit stairs. Enabled by recent single-stair code reform. It reaches ~90-95% floor efficiency (vs ~80-87% for a corridor) and gives most units windows on more than one side. Tradeoffs: more cores and elevators, and it is a newer form some lenders and reviewers are still learning.
Double-loaded corridor
The conventional layout: a central hallway with apartments on both sides and two exit stairs. Loses 15-20% of the floor to circulation.
Slab-on-grade
A building that sits on a concrete slab poured on the ground, avoiding an expensive structural podium or underground parking.
Dual-aspect unit
An apartment with windows on two or more sides, giving cross-ventilation and daylight; a quality and rent advantage.
Base case (honest floor)
The project's return built conventionally with no levers applied. The starting point everything builds from.
Lever
One real, separable move a team can make (a cost saving, a public tool, a financing structure) that changes the return.
Waterfall
The bar chart that shows the return climbing from the floor, attributing each step to a category of moves.
Levered IRR
Internal Rate of Return on invested equity, after debt: the annualized return accounting for the timing of cash in and out.
Equity multiple
Total cash returned divided by cash invested. 2.0x means you get back twice what you put in.
DSCR
Debt Service Coverage Ratio: net operating income divided by the annual loan payment. Lenders want it comfortably above 1.0x.
NOI
Net Operating Income: income after operating expenses, before debt and taxes. The number a buyer capitalizes into value.
Yield on cost
Stabilized NOI divided by total project cost; the unlevered return on the dollars spent.
Exit cap rate
The rate used to convert NOI into value at sale. A lower cap rate means a higher value.
FAR
Floor Area Ratio: total building floor area divided by land area; the zoning measure of how much you can build.
Sources & uses
The capital stack (debt, equity, subsidy) set against where it is spent (land, construction, soft costs).
LIHTC
Low-Income Housing Tax Credit: federal credits sold to investors to fund affordable rental housing; the backbone of US affordable-housing finance.
HUD 221(d)(4)
A federally insured construction-to-permanent loan with a 40-year term, high leverage, and non-recourse terms.
TIF
Tax Increment Financing: a public tool that reimburses eligible project costs from the future property-tax growth the project creates.
NMTC
New Markets Tax Credit: a federal credit that subsidizes commercial development in qualifying areas.
C-PACE
Long-term financing for energy and resiliency scope, repaid through a property assessment that stays with the building at sale.
Opportunity Zone
A federal program that defers and reduces capital-gains tax for long-term investment in designated areas.
Placemaking premium
The modest rent uplift a well-designed, amenity-rich place can earn as it matures.
Affordable sleeve
The share of homes set aside at restricted, below-market rents, often funded with LIHTC.
